# Key Insights

## Dashboard 1 — Executive Overview

### Overall Rating Performance

- The average **Overall Rating** across the dataset was **1.54**.
- The average **Food Rating** was **1.58**, while the average **Service Rating** was **1.50**.
- Food ratings were slightly higher than service ratings, with service showing comparatively lower average ratings.

### Area Setting

- Restaurants in **Closed** areas received an average Overall Rating of **1.21** across **1,034 reviews**.
- Restaurants in **Open** areas received an average Overall Rating of **1.12** across **127 reviews**.
- Closed-area restaurants accounted for approximately **89% of all reviews**, so the comparison is based on substantially more reviews for Closed areas.

### City & State Performance

- Average restaurant ratings varied across cities:
  - **Jiutepec:** 1.67
  - **Cuernavaca:** 1.63
  - **San Luis Potosi:** 1.55
  - **Ciudad Victoria:** 1.33

- At the state level:
  - **Morelos:** 1.63
  - **San Luis Potosi:** 1.55
  - **Tamaulipas:** 1.33

- The results show noticeable geographic variation in average restaurant ratings across the locations covered by the dataset.

### Price Level

- **High-price restaurants** had the highest average rating at **1.61**.
- **Medium-price restaurants** averaged **1.56**, while **Low-price restaurants** averaged **1.44**.
- In this dataset, higher price categories were associated with higher average customer ratings.

### Restaurant Policies

- Restaurants **with parking** averaged **1.48**, compared with **1.51** for restaurants **without parking**, indicating only a small difference in average ratings.
- Restaurants **with alcohol service** averaged **1.20**, compared with **1.17** for restaurants **without alcohol service**.

### Top Cuisine Insight

- **Brewery** had the highest overall rating at **2.00**, which is **66.67% above the overall average rating of 1.20** shown in the dashboard insight.
- However, this result was based on only **5 ratings**, so the high average should be interpreted with caution due to the small rating volume.

## Dashboard 4 — Market Gap Analysis

### Consumer Demand vs Restaurant Supply

- **Mexican** was the top preferred cuisine, with **97 consumer preference records**.
- Mexican represented **29.57% of consumer demand** among the full **328 preference records** analyzed.
- Mexican represented **26.80% of restaurant cuisine supply** among the **873 cuisine records** analyzed.
- This created a **+2.77 percentage-point demand-supply gap**, indicating that consumer preference share was higher than the corresponding restaurant supply share.

### Restaurant Cuisine Supply

- **Mexican** had the highest restaurant cuisine supply count among the five cuisines analyzed, with **234 records**.
- It was followed by **Bar (140)**, **Fast Food (73)**, **Cafeteria (68)**, and **Seafood (62)**.
- The supply analysis is based on **restaurant cuisine records/tags**, rather than unique restaurants.

### Demand-Supply Differences

- **Bar** had a supply share of **16.04%** compared with a demand share of **0.91%**, resulting in a **-15.12 percentage-point gap**.
- **Fast Food** had a demand share of **1.52%** and a supply share of **8.36%**, resulting in a **-6.84 percentage-point gap**.
- **Seafood** had a demand share of **0.61%** and a supply share of **7.10%**, resulting in a **-6.49 percentage-point gap**.
- **Cafeteria** had a demand share of **2.74%** and a supply share of **7.79%**, resulting in a **-5.05 percentage-point gap**.

### Rating & Market Gap

- Among the five cuisines analyzed, **Cafeteria** had the lowest average rating at **1.48**.
- Cafeteria also showed a **-5.05 percentage-point demand-supply gap**, with supply exceeding demand.
- **Mexican** had an average rating of **1.53** alongside the highest positive demand-supply gap of **+2.77 percentage points**.

### Key Market Takeaways

- **Mexican:** Demand share exceeded supply share by **2.77 pp**, making it the only cuisine among the five analyzed with a positive demand-supply gap.
- **Bar:** The largest supply-demand difference was observed here, with supply exceeding demand by **15.12 pp**.
- **Cafeteria:** Recorded the lowest average rating (**1.48**) among the five cuisines while also having a **5.05 pp supply surplus**.

## Overall Takeaways

- Restaurant ratings varied across **cities, states, price levels, and restaurant characteristics**, highlighting differences in customer-rated performance across the dataset.
- **Food ratings (1.58)** were slightly higher than **Service ratings (1.50)**, indicating a relatively lower service rating compared with food.
- The consumer analysis showed that **Students, the 18–27 age group, Medium-budget consumers, and Single consumers** accounted for a large share of the rating records.
- **Mexican** was the most preferred cuisine in the consumer preference analysis and also represented the largest cuisine supply category among the cuisines analyzed.
- The market gap analysis identified a **+2.77 percentage-point demand-supply gap for Mexican cuisine**, while **Bar** showed the largest supply surplus at **15.12 percentage points**.
- **Cafeteria** had the lowest average rating among the five cuisines analyzed at **1.48**, alongside a **5.05 percentage-point supply surplus**.
- Overall, the dashboards combine **restaurant performance, consumer behavior, cuisine preferences, and demand-supply differences** to provide a broader view of the restaurant market represented in the dataset.
